What Is BMAD?

From vague idea to shipped code — and why AI alone isn't enough

The AI Autocomplete Trap

Imagine you tell an AI coding tool: "Build me a task management app." It produces something. You ask for changes. It rewrites half the app. You ask for a feature — it breaks two others. Hours later, you're going in circles.

This isn't the AI's fault. It's the process — or the lack of one.

😵

Without structure

Each prompt starts fresh. The AI has no memory of decisions made. Requirements drift. Code contradicts itself.

🤝

With BMAD

Specialized agents guide you through each phase. Decisions are documented. Every agent knows the context. Code stays consistent.

The Big Idea: BMAD stands for Build More Architect Dreams. It's not a coding tool — it's a framework that makes AI behave like a structured team of expert collaborators, not a single overworked intern.

Why Structure Beats Raw Prompting

Think of it like building a house. You wouldn't hand a construction crew a napkin sketch and say "figure it out." You'd hire an architect first, get blueprints approved, then let builders follow a plan.

BMAD is that same pipeline — but for software, and powered entirely by AI.

📁

Artifacts that persist

Artifacts like your PRD and architecture doc are saved as files. Every AI agent reads them — so nothing is forgotten between sessions.

🎯

Agents that specialize

Each agent has a distinct role and expertise. The analyst doesn't write code. The developer doesn't second-guess requirements. Clear roles = fewer contradictions.

📐

Scale-adaptive depth

Building a quick prototype? BMAD keeps it lean. Building an enterprise system? BMAD adds the process depth you need. It adjusts automatically.

Why this helps you steer AI better: When you know the vocabulary — PRD, architecture, epics, stories — you can give AI precise instructions. Instead of "make it better," you can say "update the PRD to reflect this new requirement." That's the difference between hoping and directing.

The Four-Phase Journey

Analysis → Planning → Solutioning → Implementation — what each phase produces and why the order matters

Four Phases, One Coherent System

Software projects fail not because developers can't code — they fail because nobody agreed on what to build before building it. BMAD forces clarity up front, in four phases.

Think of it like a rocket launch: you plan the trajectory before you ignite the engines. Changing course after liftoff costs exponentially more.

🔍

Phase 1 — Analysis

Research, brainstorm, and define the idea. Output: a product brief or PRFAQ.

📋

Phase 2 — Planning

Translate the idea into detailed requirements and UX design. Output: a PRD.

🏗️

Phase 3 — Solutioning

Design the technical blueprint and break it into stories. Output: architecture doc + epics & stories.

⚙️

Phase 4 — Implementation

Code, test, review, repeat. Output: working software, sprint plan, code reviews, and retrospectives.

Phase 1: Analysis — Figure Out What You're Building

This phase is deliberately open-ended. You're not sure yet exactly what you want to build — and that's fine. The analyst agent (Mary) runs structured discovery sessions: brainstorming, market research, domain research.

1

Brainstorm

Mary facilitates a structured brainstorm using techniques like SWOT analysis. You talk, she clarifies, patterns emerge.

2

Research

Market research, technical feasibility, domain expertise. Mary looks at who else is solving this problem and how.

3

Document the idea

Output: a product brief or PRFAQ — a written artifact that captures what you're building, for whom, and why it matters. This becomes the foundation everything else builds on.

Why vibe coders love this phase: You just talk. No code, no technical decisions. You describe your vision, and the agent helps you sharpen it into something buildable. The end result is a document that lets AI understand your intentions for the entire project — not just the current conversation.

Phases 2 & 3: Clarity Before Code

These two phases transform your fuzzy idea into a precision spec. They're the most underrated parts of software development — and the ones that prevent the most bugs.

Meet the Agents

Seven distinct expert personas — each one a real specialist with a voice, a job, and strong opinions

Your AI Expert Team

Unlike a single AI that tries to do everything, BMAD gives each role to a named specialist. They have distinct personalities, communication styles, and areas of responsibility — and they stay in character throughout a session.

Think of it like a film crew. The director has a different job than the cinematographer. Giving everyone the same title and hoping for the best would be chaos.

📊

Mary — Business Analyst

Speaks like a treasure hunter. Thrilled by patterns, ruthless about ambiguity. Runs discovery sessions and produces product briefs. Phase 1.

📋

PM Agent — Product Manager

Facilitates the PRD creation process. Balances user needs, technical feasibility, and business goals. Phase 2.

🎨

UX Designer Agent

Translates requirements into user experience plans — screens, flows, interactions. Makes sure the UI plan exists before code is written. Phase 2.

🏗️

Architect Agent

Makes the technical decisions. Chooses the tech stack, designs how components connect, documents every decision. Phase 3.

💻

Amelia — Developer

"Ultra-succinct. Speaks in file paths and AC IDs." Executes stories in strict order, writes tests before marking tasks done. Phase 4.

✍️

Tech Writer Agent

Writes documentation, creates Mermaid diagrams, explains concepts, validates documents. Available anytime in any phase.

Identity block: This is literally a character description injected into the AI's instructions. It tells the AI to become Mary — not just play her, but fully embody her expertise and perspective.

Communication Style: The AI is instructed to bring energy and curiosity to every interaction — not just answer questions, but dig, probe, and celebrate discoveries alongside you.

The practical effect: When you talk to Mary, responses feel different from a generic AI chat. She'll push back on vague answers and get excited when things click into place.

How to invoke Mary: Type bmad-agent-analyst or just say "talk to Mary" in your AI IDE. She activates by loading your project config, scanning for existing artifacts, and greeting you by name.

Amelia: The Ultra-Precise Developer

Amelia is the opposite of Mary in personality — where Mary is effusive and exploratory, Amelia is terse and methodical. She has one job: execute stories correctly, with tests, in order.

AMELIA'S PRINCIPLES 
- All existing and new tests must
  pass 100% before story is ready
  for review.

- READ the entire story file
  BEFORE any implementation

- Execute tasks IN ORDER as written
  in story file — no skipping,
  no reordering

- NEVER lie about tests being
  written or passing
PLAIN ENGLISH

100% test pass rate is non-negotiable — Amelia cannot mark a story done if tests fail, even one.

She reads the whole story file before writing a single line of code. This prevents her from making assumptions about what needs to be done.

Tasks have a specific order for a reason — dependencies, data structure setup, etc. She won't skip steps even if the later steps look simpler.

The most important rule: no fabricating. If tests exist, they must actually run and pass — no "tests written" without running them.

Why this matters for you: Amelia's strict rules exist because vague developer instructions lead to vague code. When you tell AI to "add authentication," you might get code that works but skips edge cases. Amelia's constraints force structured, tested, production-quality output.

How Skills Work

The SKILL.md architecture, step-file workflows, and why "just-in-time loading" prevents AI from hallucinating your project

A Skill Is a Named Workflow You Invoke by Name

In most AI tools, you describe what you want in plain language and hope for the best. In BMAD, you invoke a named skill that follows a precise, pre-designed process.

It's like the difference between telling a contractor "make my kitchen better" versus handing them a detailed renovation plan — room by room, task by task.

📄

SKILL.md

The top-level definition. Describes what the skill does, who should use it, and (for complex workflows) points to a workflow.md file for instructions.

🗺️

workflow.md

The orchestration layer. Defines the overall goal, the agent's role, and the rules for how steps are loaded and executed.

📋

step-01.md, step-02.md...

Each step is a self-contained micro-file. The agent loads only one at a time — never reads ahead — so it can't accidentally skip steps or blend instructions together.

Why this matters for you: You can see and edit these files. If you want to change how the PRD creation interview works, you edit the step files. BMAD is a transparent, hackable system — not a black box.

The "Just-In-Time" Trick That Prevents Hallucination

Here's a subtle but critical design choice: agents load step files one at a time, only when needed. This is called just-in-time loading.

FROM workflow.md 
## Core Principles

- Micro-file Design: Each step is a
  self contained instruction file
  that must be followed exactly

- Just-In-Time Loading: Only the
  current step file is in memory
  — never load future steps until
  told to do so

- Sequential Enforcement: Sequence
  within step files must be completed
  in order, no skipping allowed
PLAIN ENGLISH

Micro-file Design: Instead of one giant instruction document, each step of a workflow is in its own small file. Smaller = more focused = less room for the AI to get confused.

Just-In-Time Loading: The AI only reads the current step. It doesn't know what step 7 says while executing step 2. This prevents it from "skipping ahead" or letting future steps contaminate its thinking about the current one.

Sequential Enforcement: Steps happen in order. No optimization, no shortcuts. The structure is the quality guarantee.

Aha! moment: AI hallucination often happens because the AI has too much context at once and starts blending things together. By giving the agent only one step at a time, BMAD dramatically reduces the surface area where things can go wrong.

The Manifest: BMAD's GPS for Skills

How does the bmad-help skill know which skills exist, what order to use them in, and which ones are required? It reads a manifest file — a CSV that catalogs every installed skill with its phase, dependencies, and required status.

module-help.csv (excerpt) 
module,skill,phase,after,required

BMad Method,
  bmad-create-prd,
  2-planning,
  "",
  true

BMad Method,
  bmad-create-architecture,
  3-solutioning,
  bmad-create-prd,
  true

BMad Method,
  bmad-validate-prd,
  2-planning,
  bmad-create-prd,
  false
PLAIN ENGLISH

bmad-create-prd: Phase 2, no prerequisites, REQUIRED. You cannot meaningfully proceed to architecture without it.

bmad-create-architecture: Phase 3, must run AFTER the PRD is done, REQUIRED. The architect needs your requirements before designing a solution.

bmad-validate-prd: Phase 2, runs after PRD creation, NOT required. Useful for catching problems in complex projects, but you can skip it for a simple prototype.

The after column creates a dependency graph. When you ask bmad-help what to do next, it reads this manifest, checks which artifacts exist in your project folder, and deduces exactly where you are in the workflow.

Party Mode & Power Moves

Navigation, shortcuts, escape hatches, and how to get unstuck when the AI hits a wall

bmad-help: Your Project GPS

The most important skill in BMAD isn't a workflow — it's a compass. bmad-help is available at any point in any phase. Ask it anything: "what should I do next?" "what phase am I in?" "how do I redo my architecture?"

It's like the GPS recalculating after you take a wrong turn. No judgment, no backtracking — just: here's where you are, here's the best route forward.

📍

Where am I?

Scans your project folder for existing artifacts and reads the manifest. Tells you exactly which phase you're in and what's been completed.

🧭

What's next?

Recommends the next skill and its menu code. If one obvious next step exists, it offers to run it for you immediately.

❓

General questions

Can't find a skill that fits? Just ask in plain language. bmad-help fetches documentation and answers from the BMAD knowledge base.

When to use bmad-help: Whenever you feel lost, stuck, or unsure what to do next. Start every new session with it if you've been away from the project. It costs nothing and catches a huge number of "I went in the wrong order" mistakes before they become problems.

Quick Dev: When You Just Want to Ship

Full BMAD workflow is powerful — but sometimes you have a clear idea and just want to build it without running through four phases. For that, there's bmad-quick-dev.

Think of it as the express lane. Full BMAD is the architect-approved construction project. Quick Dev is calling a handyman for a specific job you already know how to describe.

QUICK DEV DESCRIPTION 
name: bmad-quick-dev
description: 
  Implements any user intent,
  requirement, story, bug fix
  or change request by producing
  clean working code artifacts
  that follow the project's existing
  architecture, patterns and
  conventions.
phase: anytime
PLAIN ENGLISH

Implements any intent: No specific format required. "Fix the login bug," "add a share button," "refactor the user settings page" — all valid inputs.

Follows existing patterns: Quick Dev reads your architecture doc first (if it exists) to understand how your codebase is structured. It won't introduce random new patterns.

Phase: anytime: Available from day one — you don't have to complete other phases first. Perfect for prototyping and small standalone tasks.

Correct Course: When Plans Change

Software development rarely goes according to plan. A user test reveals your core feature doesn't resonate. A competitor launches something that changes your strategy. A technical constraint makes your architecture unworkable.

BMAD's answer is bmad-correct-course — not panic, but a structured process for navigating significant changes.

1

Describe the change

Tell the agent what changed — a new requirement, a removed feature, a pivot in direction. It analyzes the impact across all your existing artifacts.

2

Get a change proposal

The agent produces a structured proposal: which documents need updating, which stories are invalidated, whether you need to redo the architecture or just edit a few stories.

3

Execute the update

Depending on scope, you might update the PRD, redo architecture, or just revise existing stories. Correct Course routes you to the right skills in the right order.

Why this beats ad-hoc changes: When you change requirements without updating the documents, your artifacts get out of sync. Amelia will implement from the story file, which references the architecture doc, which references the PRD — if any of those contradict each other, bugs and confusion follow. Correct Course keeps everything in sync.

Standard tools give you one AI perspective. Party Mode gives you a roundtable — 2 to 4 agents independently analyzing your question from their own expertise. The architect might disagree with the analyst. The developer might push back on the PM's timeline. That's the point.

Use a third-party service. Auth is notoriously easy to get wrong. Security vulnerabilities in custom auth systems are one of the top causes of data breaches. Auth-as-a-service products have teams of security specialists. You don't.
